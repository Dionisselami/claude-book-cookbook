# Recipe 09 — Blends and hybrids

**Fixes:** genre co-ordinates that do not exist in the list, and blending that turns into mush.

## Do this

```text
get_genre_recipe("horror")     → primary: governs structure
get_genre_recipe("literary")   → secondary: governs texture
```

Then write the split down as a sentence the agent will follow:

> Structural beats are horror — the turn every three chapters, the escalating cost, the closing
> confrontation. Sentence profile and interiority are literary — long subordinate clauses, restrained
> violence, an unresolved final page. Dialogue ratio: between the two, nearer literary.

## The rule

**One genre governs structure, the other governs texture.** Structure is *when things happen*;
texture is *how they are told*. A blend that splits both evenly has no pacing and no voice.

## Useful blends

| Blend | Structure from | Texture from | What it produces |
|-------|---------------|--------------|------------------|
| Gothic romance | romance | gothic | Love story with dread as the antagonist |
| Literary mystery | mystery | literary | The crime is the occasion, not the subject |
| Comic fantasy | fantasy | comedy | Straight-faced world, absurd register |
| Tragic adventure | adventure | theatre | Momentum with a real cost |
| Children's mystery | mystery | children's | Solvable a page early, on purpose |

## Cross-genre checks

- **Stakes must match texture.** Literary restraint with thriller stakes reads as anticlimax.
- **Don't average the sentence profiles.** Pick one; a page alternating between clipped and baroque is
  a page with no voice.
- **Name the tradition you are blending into.** "Romance and horror" is a mood board. "A romance
  structured like a ghost story, in the register of *Wuthering Heights*" is a plan.

## Done when

You can state which recipe governs structure and which governs texture, and a reader could tell from
chapter 1.
