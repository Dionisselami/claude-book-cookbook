# The 11 genres, and what each one changes

Pull any of these with `get_genre_recipe("<name>")` before writing. The point of the table is what
differs *between* them — that difference is what stops every book reading the same.

| Genre | Chapters turn on | Dialogue share | Sentence profile | Ends like |
|-------|------------------|----------------|------------------|-----------|
| Theatre | Dialogue, scene, revelation in speech | Very high | Short, interruptible, spoken rhythm | Climax in the open, aftermath implied |
| Horror | Escalating intrusion; the rules of the threat | Low | Long clauses that tighten under stress | With the threat intact or transferred |
| Romance | Emotional beats: meet, walls, rupture, repair | High | Warm, interior, present-beat | Union or a deliberate refusal of it |
| Adventure | Peril and momentum; place as antagonist | Medium | Declarative, forward-moving | Cost paid, horizon open |
| Literary | Interior change; the small event that reframes | Low–medium | Long, subordinate, precise | Unresolved, but changed |
| Mystery | Clue, misdirection, deduction, reveal | High | Clipped, evidential, dialogue-forward | Explanation, then consequence |
| Gothic | Atmosphere as plot; the wrongness of a place | Low | Long, ornate, subordinate | In a state, not an event |
| Sci-fi | Extrapolation: what the premise does to people | Medium | Conceptual, precise, occasionally cold | Consequence of the idea |
| Fantasy | World-rule, quest structure, cost of power | Medium | Varies by register; rules heard in speech | Restored order, altered |
| Comedy | Escalation and the refusal of dignity | High | Timing-led, short setups, hard turns | Ambush, not resolution |
| Children's | Comprehension pace; the reader slightly ahead | High | Short, plain, concrete | Warm and safe, one real change |

## Reading the table

- **Dialogue share** is the fastest lever on genre feel. A mystery with 10% dialogue reads as literary
  fiction no matter what the cover says.
- **Sentence profile** is what models flatten first, because it has no reason to pick long clauses for
  gothic and short declaratives for thriller. The recipe does.
- **How it ends** is not decoration — the ending is the promise the whole structure is built to keep.
