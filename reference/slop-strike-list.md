# The slop strike list

The concrete tells of AI-written prose, with the replacement for each. Run it per chapter after
drafting, and state the specific cuts.

## Deferred action

| Strike | Why | Replacement |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| `seemed to`, `appeared to` | Hedges the narration; the POV character does not hedge their own perceptions | Commit: it *was*, or the character *thought* it was |
| `began to`, `started to` | Marks the start of an action instead of performing it | Delete both words: `began to walk` → `walked` |
| `could feel`, `could hear`, `could see` | Distances the reader from a sense they are already having | Name the sensation: not `she could feel the cold`, but `the cold reached her wrists` |
| `tried to` | Usually reports an attempt instead of showing it fail | Show the failure |

## Filter words

*felt, noticed, watched, saw, heard, realised, wondered, thought* — with a person doing the filtering.

> Filtered: *She noticed the door was open and felt afraid.*
> Direct: *The door was open. She stepped back.*

Keep a filter word only when the noticing *is* the event.

## Adverbs

- Stacked tag adverbs: `"…," he said quietly, angrily` → let the line carry it.
- Manner adverbs propping up weak verbs: `walked quickly` → `hurried`; `said loudly` → `shouted`.
- `very`, `really`, `quite`, `somewhat`, `rather` — delete on sight; they are almost never load
  bearing.

## Sentence-level tics

- Three-item lists used as rhythm (`dark, cold, and silent`) — cut to one or two concrete details.
- Every sentence 15–25 words. Break the run.
- Same opening across a chapter: `She…`, `The…`, `It was…`.
- Weather openings — chapters do not have to begin with the sky.
- `A [noun] of [noun]` similes stacked (`a cathedral of ice`) — one per chapter at most.
- Em-dash clauses in every paragraph, which is a voice, not a rule.
- Ending paragraphs with a short punchy sentence, every time. Vary it or it becomes a tic.

## Scene-level

- Dialogue that exists to explain the plot to a character who already knows it.
- A character restating the previous chapter's revelation for the reader.
- Interior monologue summarising the scene that just happened.
- Chapter endings that taper into reflection instead of turning.
- Scenes with no reversal: someone wants something, gets it, chapter ends.

## Structural

- The plan exists, and every chapter still has the same shape.
- Every antagonist introduced and defeated within one chapter.
- Subplots that solve themselves off-page.
- A midpoint that only adds information.
- An ending that resolves a thread introduced in the last 10%.

## How to use this file

```text
Run the strike list over chapters/09-title.md.
Output each change as: struck → replacement.
Do not restructure. Do not rewrite outside the list.
```

Mechanical, narrow, verifiable. It is the highest-value edit an agent can do to its own prose, and the
one it is most likely to skip if you do not ask for it explicitly.
